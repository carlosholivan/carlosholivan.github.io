=== 1
title: Getting to know you ¦ Conocerse
desc: Hacer preguntas y repasar el presente simple y continuo
gram: question forms ¦ present simple vs present continuous
ref: a2-present-tenses
words: personality, ambition, lifestyle, habit, usually, normal, regular, regularly, common, member, chat, contact, describe, description, opinion, interest, hobby, nowadays, especially, mostly, mainly, quite, rather, fairly, cheerful, confident, polite, honest, creative, energetic
intro:
<h3>El orden de las preguntas</h3>
<p>En inglés casi todas las preguntas siguen el mismo esquema. Apréndelo y te servirá para todos los tiempos:</p>
<table><tr><th>Palabra interrogativa</th><th>Auxiliar</th><th>Sujeto</th><th>Verbo</th><th>…</th></tr>
<tr><td>Where</td><td><strong>do</strong></td><td>you</td><td>live?</td><td></td></tr>
<tr><td>What</td><td><strong>does</strong></td><td>she</td><td>do</td><td>at weekends?</td></tr>
<tr><td>Why</td><td><strong>are</strong></td><td>they</td><td>laughing?</td><td></td></tr>
<tr><td>How often</td><td><strong>do</strong></td><td>you</td><td>go</td><td>to the gym?</td></tr></table>
<div class="tip">Con <b>be</b> y con los modales (<i>can, will…</i>) no hace falta <i>do</i>: <i>Where <b>are</b> you from? <b>Can</b> you swim?</i> Si la pregunta es por el sujeto, tampoco: <i><b>Who lives</b> here?</i> (no <i>Who does live</i>).</div>
<h3>Presente simple y presente continuo</h3>
<table><tr><th>Presente simple</th><th>Presente continuo</th></tr>
<tr><td>Rutinas, hábitos, hechos y estados permanentes</td><td>Lo que pasa ahora o en esta época (temporal)</td></tr>
<tr><td>I <strong>work</strong> in a bank.</td><td>I'<strong>m working</strong> from home this week.</td></tr>
<tr><td>She usually <strong>reads</strong> on the train.</td><td>She'<strong>s reading</strong> a great novel at the moment.</td></tr></table>
<div class="tip">Verbos de estado (<i>like, love, want, need, know, believe, understand, belong, seem</i>) no suelen ir en continuo: ✗ <i>I'm wanting</i> → ✓ <i>I want</i>.</div>
<div class="warning"><b>Errores típicos:</b><ul><li>✗ Where you live? → ✓ Where <b>do</b> you live?</li><li>✗ What does she likes? → ✓ What does she <b>like</b>?</li><li>✗ I'm living here since 2020 (se verá con el present perfect) · ✓ I live here / I've lived here since 2020</li></ul></div>
ex:
fill ¦ What ___ you usually do at the weekend? ¦ do ¦ are / does / have ¦ ¿Qué sueles hacer el fin de semana? ¦ Hábito → presente simple con do.
fill ¦ Who ___ in that house? ¦ lives ¦ does live / live / is live ¦ ¿Quién vive en esa casa? ¦ Pregunta por el sujeto: sin do.
fill ¦ I ___ a really good book at the moment. ¦ 'm reading ¦ read / reads / readed ¦ Estoy leyendo un libro muy bueno en este momento. ¦ at the moment → continuo.
fill ¦ How often ___ your brother go to the gym? ¦ does ¦ do / is / has ¦ ¿Cada cuánto va tu hermano al gimnasio? ¦ your brother = he → does.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ What does your sister do? ¦ What does your sister does? / What your sister does? / What do your sister do?
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ I need a new phone. ¦ I'm needing a new phone. / I needing a new phone. / I am need a new phone. ¦ need es un verbo de estado.
order ¦ Where do your parents live now? ¦ ¿Dónde viven ahora tus padres?
order ¦ She is working from home this week. ¦ Esta semana trabaja desde casa.
translate ¦ ¿Qué haces en tu tiempo libre? ¦ What do you do in your free time? ¦ What do you do in your spare time?
translate ¦ No entiendo esta palabra. ¦ I don't understand this word. ¦ I do not understand this word.
match ¦ polite=educado; honest=sincero; confident=seguro de sí mismo; cheerful=alegre; creative=creativo

=== 2
title: Life stories ¦ Historias de vida
desc: Contar biografías con el pasado simple
gram: past simple: regular and irregular verbs ¦ past questions ¦ time expressions
ref: a2-past-simple
words: childhood, born, grow, die, death, marry, wedding, retire, graduate, university, degree, move, leave, begin, career, famous, achieve, success, win, award, century, decade, during, later, finally, until, ago, ancient, history
intro:
<h3>El pasado simple</h3>
<p>Para hechos terminados en un momento del pasado: biografías, historias, lo que hiciste ayer. Recuerda: es la misma forma para todas las personas.</p>
<table><tr><th>Afirmativa</th><th>Negativa</th><th>Pregunta</th></tr>
<tr><td>She <strong>moved</strong> to Paris in 1990.</td><td>She <strong>didn't move</strong> to Rome.</td><td>When <strong>did</strong> she <strong>move</strong>?</td></tr>
<tr><td>He <strong>became</strong> famous.</td><td>He <strong>didn't become</strong> rich.</td><td><strong>Did</strong> he <strong>win</strong> any awards?</td></tr></table>
<h3>Irregulares frecuentes en biografías</h3>
<table><tr><td>be → was/were</td><td>become → became</td><td>begin → began</td><td>grow (up) → grew</td></tr>
<tr><td>leave → left</td><td>meet → met</td><td>win → won</td><td>write → wrote</td></tr>
<tr><td>buy → bought</td><td>teach → taught</td><td>fall → fell</td><td>spend → spent</td></tr></table>
<h3>Expresiones de tiempo</h3>
<div class="example"><strong>in</strong> 1985 · <strong>in</strong> the 1960s · <strong>in</strong> the 19th century · <strong>when</strong> she was ten · ten years <strong>ago</strong> · <strong>after</strong> university · <strong>later</strong> · <strong>finally</strong> · <strong>during</strong> the war · <strong>until</strong> 2010</div>
<div class="tip">Ortografía de -ed: <i>marry → married</i>, <i>stop → stopped</i>, <i>travel → travelled</i> (RU) / <i>traveled</i> (EE. UU.). <b>Nacer</b> es pasivo: <i>She <b>was born</b> in Lima</i>.</div>
<div class="warning"><b>Errores típicos:</b><ul><li>✗ When did she moved? → ✓ When did she <b>move</b>?</li><li>✗ He died in 1999 ago → ✓ He died in 1999 / He died 25 years ago</li><li>✗ She married with a doctor → ✓ She <b>married</b> a doctor / She got married to a doctor</li></ul></div>
ex:
fill ¦ Shakespeare ___ in 1564. ¦ was born ¦ born / is born / borned ¦ Shakespeare nació en 1564.
fill ¦ She ___ up in a small village in Wales. ¦ grew ¦ grow / growed / grown ¦ Se crió en un pueblo pequeño de Gales. ¦ grow → grew → grown.
fill ¦ When ___ he leave school? ¦ did ¦ was / does / had ¦ ¿Cuándo dejó el colegio?
fill ¦ They ___ married in 2005 and had three children. ¦ got ¦ get / were / did ¦ Se casaron en 2005 y tuvieron tres hijos. ¦ get married = casarse.
choose ¦ Pasado de «win» ¦ won ¦ winned / wan / win
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ She didn't go to university. ¦ She didn't went to university. / She not went to university. / She doesn't went to university.
order ¦ He became a famous writer in the 1960s. ¦ Se convirtió en un escritor famoso en los años sesenta.
order ¦ When did you start learning English? ¦ ¿Cuándo empezaste a aprender inglés?
translate ¦ Murió hace diez años. ¦ He died ten years ago. ¦ She died ten years ago.
translate ¦ ¿Dónde naciste? ¦ Where were you born?
match ¦ childhood=infancia; career=carrera profesional; retire=jubilarse; degree=título universitario; century=siglo

=== 3
title: What were you doing? ¦ ¿Qué estabas haciendo?
desc: El pasado continuo y el pasado simple para contar historias
gram: past continuous: was / were + -ing ¦ when / while
ref: a2-past-continuous
words: while, suddenly, sudden, happen, accident, notice, shout, scream, fall, drop, hit, break, injure, hurt, ambulance, police, storm, thunderstorm, lightning, wet, dark, frightened, frightening, crowd, lucky, luckily, unfortunately, fortunately, scene, story
intro:
<h3>El pasado continuo</h3>
<p><b>was / were + -ing</b>. Describe una acción <b>en curso</b> en un momento del pasado: el «decorado» de la historia.</p>
<table><tr><th>Afirmativa</th><th>Negativa</th><th>Pregunta</th></tr>
<tr><td>I <strong>was walking</strong> home.</td><td>We <strong>weren't watching</strong> TV.</td><td>What <strong>were</strong> you <strong>doing</strong> at 8?</td></tr></table>
<h3>Pasado continuo + pasado simple</h3>
<p>La acción larga va en continuo; la acción corta que la interrumpe, en pasado simple:</p>
<div class="example">I <strong>was having</strong> a shower <strong>when</strong> the phone <strong>rang</strong>.<br><strong>While</strong> we <strong>were walking</strong> in the park, it <strong>started</strong> to rain.</div>
<table><tr><td><strong>when</strong> + pasado simple (acción corta)</td><td>…when the phone rang.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>while</strong> + pasado continuo (acción larga)</td><td>While I was cooking, …</td></tr></table>
<div class="tip">Es como el contraste español <i>estaba duchándome / sonó el teléfono</i>. Pero el inglés <b>no</b> usa el continuo para hábitos del pasado: «de niño jugaba al fútbol» = <i>I played football / I used to play football</i> (✗ I was playing every day).</div>
<div class="warning">✗ When I was walking, I was seeing an accident. → ✓ While I was walking, I <b>saw</b> an accident. (ver, oír, saber… no van en continuo).</div>
ex:
fill ¦ I ___ dinner when you called. ¦ was cooking ¦ cooked / were cooking / am cooking ¦ Estaba haciendo la cena cuando llamaste. ¦ Acción larga interrumpida → was + -ing.
fill ¦ While they were playing, it ___ to rain. ¦ started ¦ was starting / starts / start ¦ Mientras jugaban, empezó a llover. ¦ Acción corta → pasado simple.
fill ¦ What ___ you doing at 10 o'clock last night? ¦ were ¦ was / did / are ¦ ¿Qué estabas haciendo anoche a las diez?
fill ¦ She fell and ___ her leg. ¦ broke ¦ breaked / was breaking / broken ¦ Se cayó y se rompió la pierna.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ I saw an accident while I was driving to work. ¦ I was seeing an accident while I drove to work. / I saw an accident while I drove to work. / I was seeing an accident while I was driving to work.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál completa mejor? «___ I was walking home, I met an old friend.» ¦ While ¦ When / During / Suddenly
order ¦ The sun was shining and people were swimming. ¦ Hacía sol y la gente estaba nadando.
order ¦ Suddenly we heard a loud scream. ¦ De repente oímos un grito fuerte.
translate ¦ ¿Qué estabas haciendo? ¦ What were you doing?
translate ¦ Estaba lloviendo cuando salimos. ¦ It was raining when we left. ¦ It was raining when we went out.
match ¦ suddenly=de repente; luckily=por suerte; injure=herir; crowd=multitud; storm=tormenta

=== 4
title: How do you feel? ¦ ¿Cómo te sientes?
desc: Sentimientos, adjetivos en -ed / -ing y adverbios de modo
gram: -ed / -ing adjectives ¦ adverbs of manner: quickly, well, hard
ref: a2-adverbs
words: feel, feeling, mood, amused, annoyed, annoying, anxious, disappointed, disappointing, embarrassed, embarrassing, surprised, surprising, worried, relaxed, pleased, upset, nervous, frightened, confused, tired, bored, excited, interested, angrily, happily, sadly, quietly, loudly, slowly, carefully, badly, easily, hard
intro:
<h3>Adjetivos en -ed y en -ing</h3>
<table><tr><th>-ed: cómo te sientes</th><th>-ing: lo que lo provoca</th></tr>
<tr><td>I'm <strong>bored</strong>.</td><td>The film is <strong>boring</strong>.</td></tr>
<tr><td>She was <strong>surprised</strong>.</td><td>The news was <strong>surprising</strong>.</td></tr>
<tr><td>We were <strong>disappointed</strong>.</td><td>The match was <strong>disappointing</strong>.</td></tr>
<tr><td>I felt <strong>embarrassed</strong>.</td><td>It was an <strong>embarrassing</strong> moment.</td></tr></table>
<div class="warning">«I'm boring» = soy aburrido (¡no «me aburro»!). Para decir cómo estás, usa <b>-ed</b>: <i>I'm bored, I'm interested, I'm tired</i>.</div>
<h3>Adverbios de modo</h3>
<p>Dicen <b>cómo</b> se hace algo. Casi siempre adjetivo + <b>-ly</b> y van detrás del verbo (o del objeto):</p>
<table><tr><td>quick → quick<strong>ly</strong></td><td>careful → careful<strong>ly</strong></td><td>easy → eas<strong>ily</strong></td><td>happy → happ<strong>ily</strong></td></tr>
<tr><td>irregulares: good → <strong>well</strong></td><td>fast → <strong>fast</strong></td><td>hard → <strong>hard</strong></td><td>late → <strong>late</strong></td></tr></table>
<div class="example">She speaks English <strong>very well</strong>. · Please drive <strong>carefully</strong>. · He works <strong>hard</strong>.</div>
<div class="tip">Tras <b>be, feel, look, seem, sound, taste</b> va un <b>adjetivo</b>, no un adverbio: <i>You look tired. It sounds good. I feel bad.</i> Y <i>hardly</i> no es «duramente», sino «apenas».</div>
ex:
fill ¦ I'm really ___ in history. ¦ interested ¦ interesting / interest / interestingly ¦ Me interesa mucho la historia. ¦ Cómo te sientes → -ed.
fill ¦ The end of the film was very ___ . ¦ disappointing ¦ disappointed / disappoint / disappointedly ¦ El final de la película fue muy decepcionante.
fill ¦ He speaks Japanese very ___ . ¦ well ¦ good / goodly / best ¦ Habla japonés muy bien. ¦ Adverbio de good: well.
fill ¦ Please close the door ___ . The baby is asleep. ¦ quietly ¦ quiet / quietness / quieter ¦ Cierra la puerta sin hacer ruido. El bebé está dormido.
choose ¦ «Me aburro» ¦ I'm bored. ¦ I'm boring. / I bore. / I'm boredom.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ You look tired. ¦ You look tiredly. / You look tiring. / You are looking tiredly.
order ¦ I was very embarrassed when I fell over. ¦ Me dio mucha vergüenza cuando me caí.
order ¦ She drives more carefully than her brother. ¦ Conduce con más cuidado que su hermano.
translate ¦ Estoy preocupado por el examen. ¦ I'm worried about the exam. ¦ I am worried about the exam.
translate ¦ Trabaja mucho. ¦ He works hard. ¦ She works hard.
match ¦ annoying=molesto; anxious=ansioso; upset=disgustado; relaxed=relajado; pleased=contento

=== 5
title: Money and shopping ¦ Dinero y compras
desc: Comprar, pagar y cuantificadores: a lot of, a few, a little, too much
gram: quantifiers: a lot of, a few, a little, not much / many ¦ too much / too many / enough
ref: a2-quantifiers
words: cash, coin, credit, bill, receipt, price, cost, bargain, sale, discount, cheap, expensive, afford, spend, save, earn, waste, pay, change, customer, shop assistant, department store, market, mall, budget, enough, few, plenty, several, half-price
intro:
<h3>Cuantificadores</h3>
<table><tr><th></th><th>Contables (plural)</th><th>Incontables</th></tr>
<tr><td>mucho</td><td><strong>a lot of</strong> / many shops</td><td><strong>a lot of</strong> / much money</td></tr>
<tr><td>algunos, un poco</td><td><strong>a few</strong> coins</td><td><strong>a little</strong> time</td></tr>
<tr><td>poco (negativo)</td><td><strong>not many</strong> / few people</td><td><strong>not much</strong> / little money</td></tr>
<tr><td>de sobra</td><td colspan="2"><strong>plenty of</strong> time / chairs</td></tr></table>
<div class="tip"><b>many / much</b> se usan sobre todo en negativas y preguntas; en afirmativas suena más natural <b>a lot of</b>: <i>I've got a lot of friends</i> (mejor que <i>many friends</i>).</div>
<h3>too much / too many / enough</h3>
<table><tr><td><strong>too many</strong> + contable</td><td>There are <strong>too many</strong> people here.</td><td>demasiada gente</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>too much</strong> + incontable</td><td>I spent <strong>too much</strong> money.</td><td>demasiado dinero</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>too</strong> + adjetivo</td><td>It's <strong>too expensive</strong>.</td><td>demasiado caro</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>enough</strong> + sustantivo</td><td>I haven't got <strong>enough</strong> money.</td><td>suficiente dinero</td></tr>
<tr><td>adjetivo + <strong>enough</strong></td><td>It isn't <strong>big enough</strong>.</td><td>no es lo bastante grande</td></tr></table>
<div class="warning">✗ It's enough big → ✓ It's big <b>enough</b> · ✗ too much people → ✓ too <b>many</b> people · <b>a few</b> (algunos, positivo) ≠ <b>few</b> (pocos, negativo).</div>
<div class="example">— Can I pay by card? — Sorry, cash only.<br>— Can I have a <strong>receipt</strong>, please? — Here's your <strong>change</strong> and your receipt.</div>
ex:
fill ¦ There are too ___ cars in the city centre. ¦ many ¦ much / enough / lot ¦ Hay demasiados coches en el centro. ¦ cars es contable → many.
fill ¦ I've only got a ___ money left. ¦ little ¦ few / lot / many ¦ Solo me queda un poco de dinero. ¦ money es incontable → a little.
fill ¦ This bag isn't big ___ for my laptop. ¦ enough ¦ too / much / plenty ¦ Este bolso no es lo bastante grande para mi portátil. ¦ adjetivo + enough.
fill ¦ We've got ___ of time. Don't worry. ¦ plenty ¦ many / enough / a lot ¦ Tenemos tiempo de sobra. No te preocupes.
choose ¦ «Compré unas cuantas cosas» ¦ I bought a few things. ¦ I bought a little things. / I bought few thing. / I bought a few of things.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ It's too expensive. I can't afford it. ¦ It's too much expensive. I can't afford it. / It's enough expensive. I can't afford it. / It's too expensive. I can't afford.
order ¦ Can I have a receipt, please? ¦ ¿Me da un tique, por favor?
order ¦ I spent too much money on clothes. ¦ Me gasté demasiado dinero en ropa.
translate ¦ No tengo suficiente dinero. ¦ I haven't got enough money. ¦ I don't have enough money.
translate ¦ ¡Es una ganga! ¦ It's a bargain! ¦ It is a bargain!
match ¦ receipt=tique, recibo; change=cambio (dinero); bargain=ganga; afford=permitirse; earn=ganar (dinero)

=== 6
title: Better and better ¦ Cada vez mejor
desc: Comparar personas, lugares y cosas
gram: comparative adjectives: -er, more…, better, worse ¦ as … as ¦ than
ref: a2-comparatives
words: compare, comparative, similar, different, difference, same, modern, ancient, traditional, comfortable, convenient, crowded, noisy, peaceful, safe, dangerous, lively, quiet, cheap, expensive, wide, narrow, deep, healthy, unhealthy, worse, less, than, countryside, village
intro:
<h3>Comparativos</h3>
<table><tr><th>Adjetivo</th><th>Regla</th><th>Comparativo</th></tr>
<tr><td>cheap, old, small</td><td>1 sílaba: + <b>-er</b></td><td>cheap<strong>er</strong>, old<strong>er</strong></td></tr>
<tr><td>big, hot, thin</td><td>vocal + consonante: se dobla</td><td>bi<strong>gg</strong>er, ho<strong>tt</strong>er</td></tr>
<tr><td>easy, busy, noisy</td><td>-y → <b>-ier</b></td><td>eas<strong>ier</strong>, nois<strong>ier</strong></td></tr>
<tr><td>modern, expensive</td><td>2+ sílabas: <b>more</b> + adj.</td><td><strong>more</strong> expensive</td></tr>
<tr><td>good / bad / far</td><td>irregulares</td><td><strong>better</strong> / <strong>worse</strong> / <strong>further</strong></td></tr></table>
<div class="example">London is <strong>bigger than</strong> Madrid. · The train is <strong>more comfortable than</strong> the bus. · My cold is <strong>worse</strong> today.</div>
<h3>Igualdad y otras comparaciones</h3>
<table><tr><td><strong>as</strong> … <strong>as</strong></td><td>She's <strong>as tall as</strong> her mother.</td><td>tan alta como</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>not as</strong> … <strong>as</strong></td><td>The village <strong>isn't as noisy as</strong> the city.</td><td>no tan ruidoso como</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>less</strong> + adj.</td><td>It's <strong>less expensive</strong>.</td><td>menos caro</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>much / a bit</strong> + comp.</td><td>It's <strong>much cheaper</strong>. / <strong>a bit</strong> colder</td><td>mucho / un poco más</td></tr></table>
<div class="warning"><b>Errores típicos:</b><ul><li>✗ more big → ✓ bigger · ✗ more better → ✓ better</li><li>✗ bigger that → ✓ bigger <b>than</b></li><li>✗ as tall than → ✓ as tall <b>as</b></li></ul></div>
ex:
fill ¦ The city is much ___ than the village. ¦ noisier ¦ noisyer / more noisy / noiser ¦ La ciudad es mucho más ruidosa que el pueblo. ¦ -y → -ier.
fill ¦ This sofa is ___ comfortable than the old one. ¦ more ¦ most / -er / much ¦ Este sofá es más cómodo que el viejo. ¦ Adjetivo largo → more.
fill ¦ My English is ___ than last year. ¦ better ¦ gooder / more good / best ¦ Mi inglés es mejor que el año pasado.
fill ¦ The train isn't as fast ___ the plane. ¦ as ¦ than / like / that ¦ El tren no es tan rápido como el avión.
choose ¦ Comparativo de «hot» ¦ hotter ¦ hoter / more hot / hottest
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ Life in the country is less stressful than in the city. ¦ Life in the country is less stressful that in the city. / Life in the country is more less stressful than in the city. / Life in the country is less stressfuller than in the city.
order ¦ My sister is two years older than me. ¦ Mi hermana es dos años mayor que yo.
order ¦ The weather is getting worse and worse. ¦ El tiempo está empeorando cada vez más.
translate ¦ Madrid es más grande que Valencia. ¦ Madrid is bigger than Valencia. ¦ Madrid is larger than Valencia.
translate ¦ Hoy hace un poco más de calor. ¦ It's a bit hotter today. ¦ It is a bit hotter today. / It's a bit warmer today. / Today it's a bit hotter.
match ¦ crowded=abarrotado; peaceful=tranquilo; lively=animado; convenient=práctico; narrow=estrecho

=== 7
title: The best in the world ¦ Lo mejor del mundo
desc: Los superlativos: el más alto, el mejor, el peor
gram: superlatives: the -est, the most…, the best, the worst ¦ ever / in the world
ref: a2-superlatives
words: worst, best, superlative, ever, record, unforgettable, amazing, fantastic, incredible, enormous, tiny, famous, popular, natural, wonder, desert, mountain, ocean, island, continent, planet, population, capital, national, international, tourist, sightseeing, landscape, scenery
intro:
<h3>Superlativos</h3>
<table><tr><th>Adjetivo</th><th>Comparativo</th><th>Superlativo</th></tr>
<tr><td>tall</td><td>taller</td><td><strong>the tallest</strong></td></tr>
<tr><td>big</td><td>bigger</td><td><strong>the biggest</strong></td></tr>
<tr><td>happy</td><td>happier</td><td><strong>the happiest</strong></td></tr>
<tr><td>beautiful</td><td>more beautiful</td><td><strong>the most beautiful</strong></td></tr>
<tr><td>good / bad / far</td><td>better / worse / further</td><td><strong>the best / the worst / the furthest</strong></td></tr></table>
<div class="example">Everest is <strong>the highest</strong> mountain <strong>in</strong> the world.<br>It's <strong>the best</strong> film I've <strong>ever</strong> seen.<br>That was <strong>the worst</strong> day <strong>of</strong> my life.</div>
<div class="tip">Detrás del superlativo: <b>in</b> + lugar o grupo (<i>in the world, in my class, in Spain</i>) y <b>of</b> + periodo o cantidad (<i>of my life, of the year, of all</i>). Fíjate en la estructura <i>the best … I've ever …</i> (el mejor … que he … nunca).</div>
<div class="warning">✗ the most big → ✓ the biggest · ✗ the more beautiful of the world → ✓ the most beautiful <b>in</b> the world · No olvides <b>the</b>: ✗ It's best restaurant.</div>
ex:
fill ¦ The Nile is the ___ river in the world. ¦ longest ¦ longer / most long / long ¦ El Nilo es el río más largo del mundo.
