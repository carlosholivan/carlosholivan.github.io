# B2 lessons (EOI Nivel Intermedio B2). Spanish only.

=== 1
title: Creatures of habit ¦ Animales de costumbres
desc: Hablar de hábitos y tendencias, presentes y pasados
gram: tend to ¦ will / would for habits ¦ be always + -ing (annoying habits) ¦ keep (on) + -ing
ref: b2-habits
words: tend, tendency, habit, routine, typically, invariably, generally, occasionally, seldom, rarely, frequently, constantly, annoying, irritating, nuisance, bite, nail, fingernail, snore, yawn, chew, gum, mess, untidy, punctual, punctuality, lazy, layabout, workaholic, perfectionist
intro:
<h3>Hablar de tendencias</h3>
<table><tr><td><strong>tend to</strong> + verbo</td><td>I <strong>tend to</strong> get up late at weekends. (suelo)</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>will</strong> (hábito típico, característico)</td><td>She<strong>'ll</strong> spend hours reading if you let her.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>would</strong> (hábito pasado)</td><td>As a child I <strong>would</strong> hide under the table.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>keep (on)</strong> + -ing</td><td>He <strong>keeps forgetting</strong> his keys. (no para de)</td></tr></table>
<h3>Hábitos que molestan: always + continuo</h3>
<p>Con <b>always / constantly / forever</b> el presente o el pasado continuo expresa irritación:</p>
<div class="example">You<strong>'re always leaving</strong> your socks on the floor! (¡siempre dejas…!)<br>She <strong>was constantly complaining</strong> about her job.</div>
<div class="tip">Compara: <i>He always <b>arrives</b> on time</i> (dato neutro) / <i>He's always <b>arriving</b> late!</i> (queja). El acento va en <i>always</i>.</div>
<div class="warning">«Suelo» en presente nunca es <i>use to</i>: ✗ I use to go → ✓ I <b>usually</b> go / I <b>tend to</b> go. <i>Would</i> no sirve para estados del pasado: ✗ I would have a dog → ✓ I <b>used to</b> have a dog.</div>
ex:
fill ¦ I ___ to eat too much when I'm stressed. ¦ tend ¦ use / am used / would ¦ Suelo comer demasiado cuando estoy estresado. ¦ Tendencia presente → tend to.
fill ¦ You're always ___ me! Let me finish. ¦ interrupting ¦ interrupt / interrupted / to interrupt ¦ ¡Siempre me estás interrumpiendo! Déjame terminar. ¦ always + continuo = queja.
fill ¦ My computer keeps ___ . I need a new one. ¦ crashing ¦ to crash / crash / crashed ¦ Mi ordenador no para de colgarse. Necesito uno nuevo. ¦ keep + -ing.
fill ¦ When I was little, my dad ___ read me a story every night. ¦ would ¦ will / was / tended ¦ De pequeño, mi padre me leía un cuento todas las noches. ¦ Hábito pasado → would.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál expresa irritación? ¦ He's always borrowing my things without asking. ¦ He always borrows my things. / He borrowed my things. / He tends to borrow things.
choose ¦ «De pequeña tenía un gato» ¦ I used to have a cat when I was little. ¦ I would have a cat when I was little. / I use to have a cat when I was little. / I was having a cat when I was little.
order ¦ She tends to leave everything until the last minute. ¦ Suele dejarlo todo para el último momento.
order ¦ He's forever losing his phone. ¦ Se pasa la vida perdiendo el móvil.
translate ¦ Suelo acostarme tarde. ¦ I tend to go to bed late. ¦ I usually go to bed late. / I tend to go to bed late at night.
translate ¦ ¡Siempre llegas tarde! ¦ You're always arriving late! ¦ You're always late! / You are always late! / You are always arriving late!
match ¦ workaholic=adicto al trabajo; nuisance=molestia; seldom=rara vez; invariably=invariablemente; punctual=puntual

=== 2
title: It had been raining all night ¦ Había llovido toda la noche
desc: Tiempos narrativos: el pasado perfecto continuo y los conectores de una historia
gram: past perfect simple vs continuous ¦ narrative linkers: as soon as, no sooner, by the time, eventually
ref: b2-narrative-tenses
words: narrator, plot, subplot, twist, climax, flashback, setting, reveal, revelation, suspense, mystery, gripping, thrilling, haunting, moving, heartbreaking, eventually, meanwhile, thereafter, abruptly, gradually, suddenly, instantly, realise, gasp, stagger, stare, glance, whisper, tremble
intro:
<h3>Pasado perfecto continuo</h3>
<p><b>had been + -ing</b>. Actividad que duraba <b>hasta</b> un momento del pasado (o justo antes). Pone el foco en la duración o en la causa de algo visible:</p>
<div class="example">The ground was wet. It <strong>had been raining</strong> all night.<br>She was exhausted because she <strong>had been working</strong> for twelve hours.<br>We <strong>had been walking</strong> for an hour when we realised we were lost.</div>
<table><tr><th>Past perfect simple</th><th>Past perfect continuous</th></tr>
<tr><td>acción terminada, resultado, cantidad</td><td>actividad, duración</td></tr>
<tr><td>She <strong>had written</strong> three chapters.</td><td>She <strong>had been writing</strong> since dawn.</td></tr></table>
<h3>Conectores narrativos</h3>
<table><tr><td><strong>As soon as</strong> he arrived, …</td><td>en cuanto llegó</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>By the time</strong> we got there, the shop had closed.</td><td>para cuando llegamos</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Eventually</strong>, they found the way.</td><td>al final (tras mucho tiempo)</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Meanwhile</strong>, …</td><td>mientras tanto</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>No sooner had</strong> I sat down <strong>than</strong> the phone rang.</td><td>nada más sentarme (formal, con inversión)</td></tr></table>
<div class="warning">Los verbos de estado no van en continuo: ✗ I had been knowing her → ✓ I <b>had known</b> her for years.</div>
ex:
fill ¦ Her eyes were red. She ___ crying. ¦ had been ¦ has been / was / had ¦ Tenía los ojos rojos. Había estado llorando. ¦ Causa de algo visible → past perfect continuous.
fill ¦ We had been ___ for two hours when the bus finally came. ¦ waiting ¦ waited / wait / to wait ¦ Llevábamos dos horas esperando cuando por fin llegó el autobús.
fill ¦ By the time I got home, they ___ all the cake. ¦ had eaten ¦ had been eating / have eaten / were eating ¦ Para cuando llegué a casa, se habían comido todo el pastel. ¦ Resultado terminado → simple.
fill ¦ No sooner ___ we left than it started to rain. ¦ had ¦ have / did / were ¦ Nada más salir, empezó a llover. ¦ No sooner had + sujeto + participio.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ I had known him for ten years before we became friends. ¦ I had been knowing him for ten years before we became friends. / I knew him since ten years before we became friends. / I have been knowing him for ten years.
choose ¦ «Al final encontraron la solución» ¦ Eventually they found the solution. ¦ Eventually, they would find the solution maybe. / Finally they found eventually the solution. / Actually they found the solution.
order ¦ He had been driving for hours and he was exhausted. ¦ Llevaba horas conduciendo y estaba agotado.
order ¦ As soon as she opened the door, she knew something was wrong. ¦ En cuanto abrió la puerta, supo que algo iba mal.
translate ¦ Llevaba tres años trabajando allí cuando la despidieron. ¦ She had been working there for three years when she was fired. ¦ She had been working there for three years when they fired her. / She'd been working there for three years when she was fired.
translate ¦ Había estado lloviendo. ¦ It had been raining.
match ¦ gripping=apasionante; twist=giro; flashback=analepsis; climax=clímax; stagger=tambalearse

=== 3
title: This time next year ¦ Dentro de un año
desc: Formas de futuro avanzadas
gram: future continuous: will be + -ing ¦ future perfect: will have + participle ¦ be about to, be due to, be bound to, be on the point of
ref: b2-future-forms
words: forecast, prospect, outlook, foresee, anticipate, predictable, unpredictable, inevitable, bound, due, imminent, forthcoming, eventually, deadline, schedule, scheduled, retirement, pension, career, graduate, mortgage, settle down, long-term, short-term, goal, ambition, achievable, milestone, onwards
intro:
<h3>Futuro continuo</h3>
<p><b>will be + -ing</b>: algo que <b>estará en curso</b> en un momento futuro; también planes que siguen su curso normal:</p>
<div class="example">This time tomorrow I<strong>'ll be lying</strong> on a beach. (estaré tumbado)<br><strong>Will</strong> you <strong>be using</strong> the car tonight? (pregunta educada por los planes)</div>
<h3>Futuro perfecto</h3>
<p><b>will have + participio</b>: algo que <b>ya habrá terminado</b> antes de un momento futuro, normalmente con <b>by</b>:</p>
<div class="example"><strong>By</strong> 2030, they <strong>will have finished</strong> the new airport.<br>By the time you arrive, I<strong>'ll have cooked</strong> dinner.</div>
<h3>Otras expresiones de futuro</h3>
<table><tr><td><strong>be about to</strong> + verbo</td><td>The film <strong>is about to</strong> start. (está a punto de)</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>be on the point of</strong> + -ing</td><td>I was <strong>on the point of</strong> leaving when she called.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>be due to</strong> + verbo</td><td>The train <strong>is due to</strong> arrive at 6. (está previsto)</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>be bound to</strong> + verbo</td><td>It<strong>'s bound to</strong> rain. (seguro que…)</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>be likely / unlikely to</strong></td><td>Prices <strong>are likely to</strong> rise.</td></tr></table>
<div class="warning">Tras <b>by the time, when, as soon as, until</b>, presente aunque hable del futuro: ✗ by the time you will arrive → ✓ by the time you <b>arrive</b>.</div>
ex:
fill ¦ Don't call at nine. We'll ___ dinner then. ¦ be having ¦ have / having / have had ¦ No llames a las nueve. Estaremos cenando. ¦ En curso en un momento futuro → will be + -ing.
fill ¦ By the end of the year, I'll ___ enough money for the trip. ¦ have saved ¦ be saving / save / saved ¦ Para final de año, habré ahorrado suficiente dinero para el viaje. ¦ Terminado antes de un momento → will have + participio.
fill ¦ Hurry up! The train is ___ to leave. ¦ about ¦ due / bound / point ¦ ¡Date prisa! El tren está a punto de salir.
fill ¦ She's worked so hard. She's ___ to pass. ¦ bound ¦ about / due / sure of ¦ Ha trabajado muchísimo. Seguro que aprueba. ¦ be bound to = seguro que.
choose ¦ «El avión debe aterrizar a las 10» ¦ The plane is due to land at 10. ¦ The plane is bound to land at 10. / The plane is about land at 10. / The plane will be due land at 10.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ I'll call you as soon as I've finished. ¦ I'll call you as soon as I'll finish. / I'll call you as soon as I will have finished. / I call you as soon as I'll have finished.
order ¦ By next June I will have lived here for ten years. ¦ En junio hará diez años que vivo aquí.
order ¦ Will you be going to the meeting tomorrow? ¦ ¿Vas a ir a la reunión de mañana?
translate ¦ Estaba a punto de llamarte. ¦ I was about to call you. ¦ I was on the point of calling you. / I was just about to call you.
translate ¦ Mañana a esta hora estaré volando a Tokio. ¦ This time tomorrow I'll be flying to Tokyo. ¦ This time tomorrow I will be flying to Tokyo. / By this time tomorrow I'll be flying to Tokyo.
match ¦ milestone=hito; imminent=inminente; settle down=sentar la cabeza; outlook=perspectiva; foresee=prever

=== 4
title: You should have told me ¦ Me lo deberías haber dicho
desc: Modales en pasado: deducciones, críticas y arrepentimientos
gram: must have / might have / can't have + participle ¦ should have / ought to have ¦ needn't have / didn't need to ¦ could have
ref: b2-modal-perfects
words: speculate, speculation, assume, assumption, deduce, deduction, presumably, apparently, evidently, doubtful, misunderstanding, misunderstand, overlook, overhear, oversleep, forgetful, careless, carelessly, thoughtless, regret, reproach, criticise, blame, excuse, apologise, mistake, wrongly, rightly, consequence, hindsight
intro:
<h3>Deducir sobre el pasado</h3>
<table><tr><td>casi seguro que sí</td><td><strong>must have</strong> + participio</td><td>The streets are wet. It <strong>must have rained</strong>.</td></tr>
<tr><td>posible</td><td><strong>might / may / could have</strong></td><td>She <strong>might have missed</strong> the bus.</td></tr>
<tr><td>casi seguro que no</td><td><strong>can't / couldn't have</strong></td><td>He <strong>can't have seen</strong> us. He didn't say hello.</td></tr></table>
<h3>Criticar y lamentar</h3>
<table><tr><td><strong>should have / ought to have</strong></td><td>lo correcto habría sido (no se hizo)</td><td>You <strong>should have told</strong> me!</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>shouldn't have</strong></td><td>se hizo, y fue un error</td><td>I <strong>shouldn't have eaten</strong> so much.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>could have</strong></td><td>posibilidad no aprovechada; reproche</td><td>You <strong>could have</strong> hurt yourself! You <strong>could have</strong> called.</td></tr></table>
<h3>needn't have / didn't need to</h3>
<div class="example">I <strong>needn't have brought</strong> an umbrella. (lo traje y no hacía falta)<br>I <strong>didn't need to</strong> bring an umbrella. (no hacía falta; normalmente no lo traje)</div>
<div class="warning">En la conversación suenan reducidos: <i>should've, must've, could've</i> /ʃʊdəv/. Por eso los nativos a veces escriben mal «should of». ✗ must had → ✓ must <b>have</b> + participio. Fíjate: «debió de llover» = <i>it must have rained</i>; «debería haber llamado» = <i>I should have called</i>.</div>
ex:
fill ¦ The lights are off. They must ___ to bed. ¦ have gone ¦ had gone / gone / have go ¦ Las luces están apagadas. Deben de haberse ido a la cama. ¦ Deducción segura → must have + participio.
fill ¦ He can't ___ the letter. It's still here. ¦ have sent ¦ has sent / sent / have send ¦ No puede haber enviado la carta. Sigue aquí. ¦ Deducción negativa → can't have.
fill ¦ You ___ have told me it was a formal party! ¦ should ¦ must / would / can ¦ ¡Me deberías haber dicho que era una fiesta formal! ¦ Reproche → should have.
fill ¦ I ___ have cooked so much. Nobody was hungry. ¦ needn't ¦ mustn't / couldn't / shouldn't to ¦ No hacía falta que cocinara tanto. Nadie tenía hambre. ¦ Lo hice y no era necesario → needn't have.
choose ¦ «Puede que se le haya olvidado» ¦ She might have forgotten. ¦ She might forgot. / She can have forgotten. / She must forgotten.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ You could have hurt yourself! ¦ You could hurt yourself yesterday! / You could had hurt yourself! / You could of hurt yourself!
order ¦ We should have booked a table. ¦ Deberíamos haber reservado mesa.
order ¦ They must have got lost on the way. ¦ Se deben de haber perdido por el camino.
translate ¦ No debería haberlo dicho. ¦ I shouldn't have said it. ¦ I should not have said it. / I shouldn't have said that. / I should not have said that.
translate ¦ Debió de costar mucho dinero. ¦ It must have cost a lot of money. ¦ It must have been very expensive. / It must have cost a lot.
match ¦ hindsight=retrospectiva; presumably=probablemente; overlook=pasar por alto; oversleep=quedarse dormido; reproach=reproche

=== 5
title: If things had been different ¦ Si las cosas hubieran sido distintas
desc: Condicionales mixtos y alternativas a if
gram: mixed conditionals ¦ unless, provided (that), as long as, suppose, otherwise, even if ¦ inverted conditionals (Should you…, Had I known…)
ref: b2-mixed-conditionals
words: condition, conditional, provided, providing, supposing, otherwise, unless, even if, whether, alternatively, hypothetical, scenario, outcome, consequence, decisive, crucial, turning point, opportunity, chance, fate, luck, coincidence, regret, pursue, abandon, emigrate, inherit, accept, decline, risk
intro:
<h3>Condicionales mixtos</h3>
<p>Combinan tiempos distintos en la condición y en el resultado:</p>
<table><tr><th>Condición pasada → resultado presente</th><th>Condición presente → resultado pasado</th></tr>
<tr><td>If I <strong>had taken</strong> that job, I<strong>'d be</strong> rich now.</td><td>If I <strong>weren't</strong> so shy, I<strong>'d have spoken</strong> to her.</td></tr>
<tr><td>(if + past perfect, would + verbo)</td><td>(if + past simple, would have + participio)</td></tr></table>
<h3>Alternativas a if</h3>
<table><tr><td><strong>unless</strong></td><td>a menos que</td><td>I won't go <strong>unless</strong> you come.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>provided / providing (that), as long as</strong></td><td>siempre que</td><td>You can borrow it <strong>as long as</strong> you're careful.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>suppose / supposing</strong></td><td>¿y si…?, supongamos que</td><td><strong>Suppose</strong> you lost your job, what would you do?</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>even if</strong></td><td>aunque (incluso si)</td><td>I'll go <strong>even if</strong> it rains.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>otherwise</strong></td><td>si no, de lo contrario</td><td>Hurry, <strong>otherwise</strong> we'll be late.</td></tr></table>
<h3>Condicionales con inversión (formal)</h3>
<div class="example"><strong>Should</strong> you need help, call me. (= If you should need…)<br><strong>Had</strong> I known, I would have come. (= If I had known…)<br><strong>Were</strong> I in your position, I'd resign. (= If I were…)</div>
<div class="warning">✗ Unless you don't hurry → ✓ <b>Unless</b> you hurry (unless ya es negativo). <i>even if</i> ≠ <i>even though</i>: <i>even though</i> = aunque (es un hecho).</div>
ex:
fill ¦ If I had studied medicine, I ___ a doctor now. ¦ would be ¦ would have been / will be / had been ¦ Si hubiera estudiado Medicina, ahora sería médico. ¦ Condición pasada, resultado presente.
fill ¦ If she ___ so stubborn, she would have apologised. ¦ weren't ¦ hadn't been so / wasn't being / isn't ¦ Si no fuera tan terca, se habría disculpado. ¦ Rasgo presente, resultado pasado → if + past simple.
fill ¦ I'll lend you the money ___ you pay me back next week. ¦ provided ¦ unless / otherwise / even ¦ Te presto el dinero siempre que me lo devuelvas la semana que viene.
fill ¦ ___ I known about the problem, I would have helped. ¦ Had ¦ If / Have / Should ¦ De haber sabido lo del problema, habría ayudado. ¦ Inversión: Had + sujeto + participio.
choose ¦ «No iré a menos que me inviten» ¦ I won't go unless they invite me. ¦ I won't go unless they don't invite me. / I don't go unless they will invite me. / I won't go if they invite me.
choose ¦ «Si necesita algo, llámeme» (formal) ¦ Should you need anything, please call me. ¦ Would you need anything, please call me. / Should you to need anything, please call me. / If should you need anything, call me.
order ¦ If I hadn't missed that train, we would never have met. ¦ Si no hubiera perdido aquel tren, nunca nos habríamos conocido.
order ¦ Take a map, otherwise you'll get lost. ¦ Llévate un mapa; si no, te perderás.
translate ¦ Iré aunque llueva. ¦ I'll go even if it rains. ¦ I will go even if it rains.
translate ¦ Si hubiera nacido en Londres, ahora hablaría inglés perfectamente. ¦ If I had been born in London, I would speak perfect English now. ¦ If I'd been born in London, I'd speak perfect English now. / If I had been born in London, I'd speak perfect English now. / If I had been born in London, I would speak English perfectly now.
match ¦ turning point=momento decisivo; supposing=suponiendo que; hypothetical=hipotético; decline=rechazar; inherit=heredar

=== 6
title: It's time we left ¦ Ya es hora de irnos
desc: El pasado irreal: wish, if only, I'd rather, it's time
gram: wish + would ¦ I'd rather + past ¦ it's (high) time + past ¦ as if / as though
ref: b2-unreal-past
words: wish, if only, rather, high time, as if, as though, preference, prefer, complain, complaint, nag, criticise, behave, behaviour, manners, rude, inconsiderate, selfish, noisy, nuisance, tolerate, put up with, fed up, sick of, annoyed, irritated, frustrated, patient, impatient, temper
intro:
<h3>wish + would</h3>
<p>Para quejarse de lo que <b>otra persona</b> hace (o no hace) y nos gustaría que cambiara:</p>
<div class="example">I <strong>wish</strong> you <strong>would</strong> stop interrupting me. (ojalá dejaras de…)<br>I <strong>wish</strong> it <strong>would</strong> stop raining.</div>
<table><tr><td>wish + <b>pasado</b></td><td>situación presente irreal</td><td>I wish I <strong>lived</strong> nearer.</td></tr>
<tr><td>wish + <b>past perfect</b></td><td>arrepentimiento pasado</td><td>I wish I <strong>hadn't gone</strong>.</td></tr>
<tr><td>wish + <b>would</b></td><td>queja, deseo de cambio de otro</td><td>I wish he <strong>would</strong> listen.</td></tr></table>
<div class="warning">No se usa <i>wish + would</i> con uno mismo: ✗ I wish I would… → ✓ I wish I <b>could</b>… Para deseos posibles de futuro se usa <b>hope</b>: <i>I <b>hope</b> you pass</i> (✗ I wish you pass).</div>
<h3>I'd rather · It's time · as if</h3>
<table><tr><td><strong>I'd rather</strong> + forma base (yo)</td><td>I'd rather <strong>stay</strong> in.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>I'd rather</strong> + otra persona + <b>pasado</b></td><td>I'd rather you <strong>didn't smoke</strong> here. (preferiría que no fumaras)</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>It's (high) time</strong> + sujeto + <b>pasado</b></td><td>It's time we <strong>went</strong> home. It's high time you <strong>found</strong> a job.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>as if / as though</strong> + pasado (irreal)</td><td>He talks <strong>as if</strong> he <strong>knew</strong> everything.</td></tr></table>
<div class="tip">Todas estas estructuras usan el pasado con valor <b>irreal</b>, igual que el español usa el imperfecto de subjuntivo: «preferiría que no <i>fumaras</i>», «ya es hora de que nos <i>fuéramos</i>».</div>
ex:
fill ¦ I wish my neighbours ___ make so much noise. ¦ wouldn't ¦ don't / won't / didn't use to ¦ Ojalá mis vecinos no hicieran tanto ruido. ¦ Queja sobre otros → wish + would.
fill ¦ It's time we ___ home. It's very late. ¦ went ¦ go / will go / have gone ¦ Ya es hora de irnos a casa. Es muy tarde. ¦ it's time + pasado.
fill ¦ I'd rather you ___ tell anyone about this. ¦ didn't ¦ don't / won't / not ¦ Preferiría que no se lo contaras a nadie. ¦ I'd rather + persona + pasado.
fill ¦ He spends money as if he ___ a millionaire. ¦ were ¦ is / would be / has been ¦ Gasta dinero como si fuera millonario. ¦ as if + pasado irreal.
choose ¦ «Espero que tengas un buen viaje» ¦ I hope you have a good trip. ¦ I wish you have a good trip. / I wish you would have a good trip. / I hope you would have a good trip.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ I wish I could speak French. ¦ I wish I would speak French. / I wish I can speak French. / I wish I will speak French.
order ¦ I wish you would listen to me for once. ¦ Ojalá me escucharas por una vez.
order ¦ It's high time the government did something. ¦ Ya va siendo hora de que el gobierno haga algo.
translate ¦ Prefiero quedarme en casa. ¦ I'd rather stay at home. ¦ I would rather stay at home. / I'd rather stay home. / I'd prefer to stay at home.
translate ¦ Ojalá dejara de llover. ¦ I wish it would stop raining. ¦ If only it would stop raining.
match ¦ put up with=aguantar; fed up=harto; nag=dar la lata; inconsiderate=desconsiderado; temper=genio

=== 7
title: It is said that… ¦ Se dice que…
desc: La pasiva avanzada: impersonal, con dos objetos y causativa
gram: impersonal passive: It is said that… / He is said to… ¦ passive with two objects ¦ have / get something done ¦ be supposed to
ref: b2-passive
words: allege, allegedly, reportedly, supposedly, rumour, claim, believe, consider, estimate, report, suspect, expect, know, rumoured, alleged, suspect, investigate, investigation, evidence, witness, trial, jury, judge, sentence, convict, prison, release, arrest, charge, accuse
intro:
<h3>Pasiva impersonal</h3>
<p>Típica de las noticias, para opiniones y rumores sin decir quién los dice:</p>
<table><tr><th>It + pasiva + that</th><th>Sujeto + pasiva + to-infinitivo</th></tr>
<tr><td><strong>It is said that</strong> he is very rich.</td><td>He <strong>is said to be</strong> very rich.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>It is believed that</strong> the thief escaped.</td><td>The thief <strong>is believed to have escaped</strong>.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>It is expected that</strong> prices will rise.</td><td>Prices <strong>are expected to rise</strong>.</td></tr></table>
<div class="tip">Si lo dicho es <b>anterior</b> al momento en que se dice, usa el <b>infinitivo perfecto</b>: <i>She is thought <b>to have left</b> the country</i> (se cree que se ha ido). Verbos típicos: <i>say, believe, think, know, report, expect, consider, estimate, allege</i>.</div>
