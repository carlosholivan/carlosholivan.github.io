# B1 lessons (EOI Nivel Intermedio B1). Spanish only.

=== 1
title: Questions, questions ¦ Preguntas y más preguntas
desc: Preguntas de sujeto y de objeto, preguntas indirectas y question tags
gram: subject and object questions ¦ indirect questions: Could you tell me…? ¦ question tags
ref: b1-questions
words: questionnaire, interviewee, curious, curiosity, inquiry, wonder, reply, respond, confirm, assume, suppose, personally, honestly, obviously, apparent, talkative, easygoing, stubborn, selfish, generous, jealous, ambitious, sensitive, reliable, loyal, hard-working, moody, organised, cautious, enthusiastic
intro:
<h3>Preguntas de sujeto y de objeto</h3>
<p>Si la palabra interrogativa es el <b>objeto</b>, usamos auxiliar (do/does/did). Si es el <b>sujeto</b>, no:</p>
<table><tr><th>Pregunta de objeto</th><th>Pregunta de sujeto</th></tr>
<tr><td>Who <strong>did</strong> you call? (Llamaste a alguien: ¿a quién?)</td><td>Who <strong>called</strong> you? (Alguien te llamó: ¿quién?)</td></tr>
<tr><td>What <strong>did</strong> she say?</td><td>What <strong>happened</strong>?</td></tr>
<tr><td>Which team <strong>do</strong> you support?</td><td>Which team <strong>won</strong>?</td></tr></table>
<div class="warning">✗ What did happen? → ✓ What <b>happened</b>? · ✗ Who did write this? → ✓ Who <b>wrote</b> this?</div>
<h3>Preguntas indirectas</h3>
<p>Son más educadas. Tras la introducción, el orden es el de una frase <b>afirmativa</b> y desaparece do/does/did:</p>
<table><tr><th>Directa</th><th>Indirecta</th></tr>
<tr><td>Where <strong>is the station</strong>?</td><td>Could you tell me where <strong>the station is</strong>?</td></tr>
<tr><td>What time <strong>does it open</strong>?</td><td>Do you know what time <strong>it opens</strong>?</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Is</strong> she coming?</td><td>I wonder <strong>if / whether</strong> she's coming.</td></tr></table>
<h3>Question tags</h3>
<p>Pequeñas preguntas al final para confirmar («¿verdad?», «¿no?»). Afirmativa → tag negativo y al revés, con el mismo auxiliar:</p>
<div class="example">You're Spanish, <strong>aren't you</strong>? · She lives here, <strong>doesn't she</strong>? · They didn't come, <strong>did they</strong>? · You can swim, <strong>can't you</strong>? · I'm late, <strong>aren't I</strong>?</div>
ex:
fill ¦ Who ___ the window? — Tom did. ¦ broke ¦ did break / did broke / breaked ¦ ¿Quién rompió la ventana? —Tom. ¦ Pregunta de sujeto: sin did.
fill ¦ Could you tell me where the toilets ___ ? ¦ are ¦ are they / do are / is ¦ ¿Podría decirme dónde están los servicios? ¦ Pregunta indirecta: orden afirmativo.
fill ¦ You live near here, ___ you? ¦ don't ¦ do / aren't / isn't ¦ Vives cerca de aquí, ¿no? ¦ Afirmativa con presente → don't.
fill ¦ I wonder ___ she'll come to the party. ¦ if ¦ that / what / does ¦ Me pregunto si vendrá a la fiesta.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ What happened at the meeting? ¦ What did happen at the meeting? / What happened at the meeting did? / What was happened at the meeting?
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ Do you know what time the bank opens? ¦ Do you know what time does the bank open? / Do you know what time opens the bank? / Do you know what time is the bank open?
order ¦ Who did you meet at the party? ¦ ¿A quién conociste en la fiesta?
order ¦ She hasn't finished yet, has she? ¦ Todavía no ha terminado, ¿verdad?
translate ¦ ¿Sabes dónde vive? ¦ Do you know where she lives? ¦ Do you know where he lives?
translate ¦ ¿Quién te lo dijo? ¦ Who told you? ¦ Who told you that? / Who told you it?
match ¦ stubborn=terco; reliable=fiable; selfish=egoísta; easygoing=de trato fácil; talkative=hablador

=== 2
title: Stories ¦ Historias
desc: Los tiempos narrativos: pasado simple, continuo y pasado perfecto
gram: past perfect: had + participle ¦ narrative tenses ¦ by the time, before, after, already
ref: b1-narrative-tenses
words: narrate, narrative, tale, storyteller, plot, character, setting, sequence, eventually, meanwhile, previous, former, realize, recall, discover, reveal, suspect, witness, mystery, detective, thief, burglar, theft, crime, criminal, arrest, escape, disappear, shocked, terrified
intro:
<h3>El pasado perfecto</h3>
<p><b>had + participio</b>. Se usa para dejar claro que algo ocurrió <b>antes</b> de otro momento del pasado:</p>
<div class="example">When I got to the station, the train <strong>had left</strong>. (primero se fue el tren, luego llegué yo)<br>She was nervous because she <strong>had never flown</strong> before.</div>
<table><tr><th>Pasado simple</th><th>Pasado perfecto</th></tr>
<tr><td>When we arrived, they <strong>started</strong> eating. (llegamos → empezaron)</td><td>When we arrived, they <strong>had started</strong> eating. (ya habían empezado)</td></tr></table>
<h3>Los tres tiempos narrativos juntos</h3>
<table><tr><td><b>pasado continuo</b></td><td>el fondo, la escena</td><td>It <strong>was raining</strong> and people <strong>were hurrying</strong> home.</td></tr>
<tr><td><b>pasado simple</b></td><td>los hechos principales, en orden</td><td>Suddenly a man <strong>ran</strong> out of the bank.</td></tr>
<tr><td><b>pasado perfecto</b></td><td>lo que había pasado antes</td><td>He <strong>had stolen</strong> €10,000.</td></tr></table>
<div class="tip">Expresiones típicas: <i>by the time</i> (para cuando), <i>already</i> (ya), <i>before</i>, <i>after</i>, <i>as soon as</i>, <i>until</i>. Con <i>after</i> y <i>before</i> el orden ya está claro y se puede usar pasado simple: <i>After he (had) finished, he left.</i></div>
<div class="warning">El español también tiene «había + participio», así que el uso es muy parecido. El error típico es en la forma: ✗ When I arrived, the film has started → ✓ <b>had</b> started.</div>
ex:
fill ¦ When I got home, someone ___ into my flat. ¦ had broken ¦ has broken / broke / was breaking ¦ Cuando llegué a casa, alguien había entrado a robar en mi piso. ¦ Antes de llegar → past perfect.
fill ¦ I ___ that film before, so I didn't go. ¦ had seen ¦ saw / have seen / was seeing ¦ Ya había visto esa película, así que no fui.
fill ¦ While she ___ in the park, she found a wallet. ¦ was walking ¦ had walked / walked / walks ¦ Mientras paseaba por el parque, encontró una cartera. ¦ Escena → pasado continuo.
fill ¦ By the time the police arrived, the thief ___ . ¦ had escaped ¦ escaped / has escaped / was escaping ¦ Para cuando llegó la policía, el ladrón se había escapado.
choose ¦ «Había olvidado las llaves» ¦ I had forgotten my keys. ¦ I have forgotten my keys before. / I had forgot my keys. / I was forgotten my keys.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ She realised she had left her phone on the bus. ¦ She realised she has left her phone on the bus. / She had realised she left her phone on the bus before. / She was realising she had leave her phone on the bus.
order ¦ We had never seen such a beautiful place. ¦ Nunca habíamos visto un sitio tan bonito.
order ¦ The film had already started when we arrived. ¦ La película ya había empezado cuando llegamos.
translate ¦ Cuando llegué, ya se habían ido. ¦ When I arrived, they had already left. ¦ When I arrived they had already left. / When I got there, they had already left. / When I arrived, they had already gone.
translate ¦ No había estado nunca en Londres. ¦ I had never been to London. ¦ I'd never been to London. / I had never been in London.
match ¦ witness=testigo; burglar=ladrón; reveal=revelar; plot=argumento; eventually=al final

=== 3
title: The good old days ¦ Los buenos tiempos
desc: Hábitos y estados del pasado con used to y would
gram: used to / didn't use to ¦ would for past habits ¦ be used to / get used to
ref: b1-used-to
words: used to, childhood, generation, nowadays, memory, memorable, remind, remember, recall, era, former, old-fashioned, traditional, traditionally, gradually, habit, routine, typical, typically, neighbourhood, primary school, secondary school, toy, pocket money, strict, adapt, accustom, change, miss, lifestyle
intro:
<h3>used to</h3>
<p>Hábitos y estados del pasado que <b>ya no</b> son así. Equivale a «solía» o al imperfecto español:</p>
<table><tr><th>Afirmativa</th><th>Negativa</th><th>Pregunta</th></tr>
<tr><td>I <strong>used to</strong> live in Seville.</td><td>I <strong>didn't use to</strong> like vegetables.</td><td><strong>Did</strong> you <strong>use to</strong> play outside?</td></tr></table>
<div class="tip">Con <i>didn't</i> y <i>did</i> se escribe <b>use to</b> (sin -d). Para un hábito presente no existe <i>use to</i>: «suelo levantarme temprano» = <i>I <b>usually</b> get up early</i> (✗ I use to get up).</div>
<h3>would para hábitos del pasado</h3>
<p>En narraciones, <b>would</b> también expresa acciones repetidas del pasado, pero <b>no</b> estados:</p>
<div class="example">Every summer we <strong>would</strong> go to the beach and my grandfather <strong>would</strong> tell us stories. ✓<br>✗ We would live in a small house. → ✓ We <strong>used to</strong> live in a small house. (estado)</div>
<h3>be used to / get used to + -ing</h3>
<table><tr><td><strong>be used to</strong> + -ing / sustantivo</td><td>estar acostumbrado a</td><td>I'm used to getting up early.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>get used to</strong> + -ing / sustantivo</td><td>acostumbrarse a</td><td>I can't get used to the cold.</td></tr></table>
<div class="warning">No confundas: <i>I <b>used to</b> drive</i> (antes conducía) / <i>I'm <b>used to</b> driving</i> (estoy acostumbrado a conducir).</div>
ex:
fill ¦ I ___ to play the piano when I was a child. ¦ used ¦ use / was used / am used ¦ De niño tocaba el piano. ¦ Hábito pasado → used to.
fill ¦ Did you ___ to wear a uniform at school? ¦ use ¦ used / using / uses ¦ ¿Llevabas uniforme en el colegio? ¦ Con did → use to.
fill ¦ Now I live in London, but I'm not used to ___ on the left. ¦ driving ¦ drive / drove / driven ¦ Ahora vivo en Londres, pero no estoy acostumbrado a conducir por la izquierda. ¦ be used to + -ing.
fill ¦ Every Sunday my grandmother ___ make a big lunch for everyone. ¦ would ¦ was / used / did ¦ Todos los domingos mi abuela preparaba una gran comida para todos. ¦ Hábito repetido → would.
choose ¦ «Suelo ir al gimnasio por la mañana» ¦ I usually go to the gym in the morning. ¦ I use to go to the gym in the morning. / I used to go to the gym in the morning. / I'm used to go to the gym in the morning.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ This town used to be very quiet. ¦ This town would be very quiet. / This town use to be very quiet. / This town was used to be very quiet.
order ¦ We used to spend every summer at the beach. ¦ Pasábamos todos los veranos en la playa.
order ¦ It took me a long time to get used to the weather. ¦ Me costó mucho acostumbrarme al clima.
translate ¦ Antes tenía el pelo largo. ¦ I used to have long hair. ¦ I used to have got long hair.
translate ¦ Estoy acostumbrado al ruido. ¦ I'm used to the noise. ¦ I am used to the noise.
match ¦ nowadays=hoy en día; old-fashioned=anticuado; gradually=poco a poco; pocket money=paga; adapt=adaptarse

=== 4
title: Experiences and achievements ¦ Experiencias y logros
desc: Present perfect o pasado simple: cuándo usar cada uno
gram: present perfect vs past simple ¦ ever, never, so far, recently ¦ been vs gone
ref: b1-perfect-vs-past
words: achievement, achieve, accomplish, success, successful, failure, attempt, manage, progress, so far, recently, lately, ever, experience, experienced, opportunity, challenge, challenging, goal, aim, ambition, prize, award, medal, championship, record, career, qualification, qualified, impressive, proud
intro:
<h3>¿Present perfect o pasado simple?</h3>
<table><tr><th>Present perfect (have/has + participio)</th><th>Pasado simple</th></tr>
<tr><td>El momento <b>no se dice o no importa</b>; periodo que llega hasta ahora</td><td>Momento <b>terminado</b> y concreto</td></tr>
<tr><td>I<strong>'ve been</strong> to Japan three times.</td><td>I <strong>went</strong> to Japan in 2019.</td></tr>
<tr><td>She<strong>'s won</strong> two medals this year.</td><td>She <strong>won</strong> a medal last year.</td></tr>
<tr><td>ever, never, yet, already, just, so far, recently, this week, today</td><td>yesterday, last…, … ago, in 2010, when I was young</td></tr></table>
<div class="tip">Truco: si puedes preguntar <b>When?</b> y la respuesta está en la frase, usa pasado simple. Las preguntas con <b>When</b> van siempre en pasado simple: ✓ <i>When did you arrive?</i> (✗ When have you arrived?).</div>
<div class="warning"><b>Diferencias con el español peninsular:</b> «Hoy he visto a Juan» = <i>I've seen Juan today</i> (igual). Pero «Esta mañana he ido al médico», dicho por la tarde = <i>This morning I <b>went</b> to the doctor</i> (la mañana ya terminó). Y en la biografía de alguien que ha muerto, siempre pasado simple: <i>Picasso <b>painted</b> many pictures</i>.</div>
<div class="example">— Have you ever run a marathon? — Yes, I have. I ran the Valencia marathon last year. It took me four hours.</div>
ex:
fill ¦ I ___ my keys. Can you help me look for them? ¦ 've lost ¦ lost / had lost / was losing ¦ He perdido las llaves. ¿Me ayudas a buscarlas? ¦ Resultado presente → present perfect.
fill ¦ We ___ to Paris in 2018. ¦ went ¦ have gone / have been / had gone ¦ Fuimos a París en 2018. ¦ in 2018 → pasado simple.
fill ¦ So far, she ___ three races this season. ¦ has won ¦ won / wins / had won ¦ Hasta ahora ha ganado tres carreras esta temporada. ¦ so far → present perfect.
fill ¦ When ___ you start working here? ¦ did ¦ have / had / do ¦ ¿Cuándo empezaste a trabajar aquí? ¦ Preguntas con when → pasado simple.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ I saw him two days ago. ¦ I have seen him two days ago. / I've saw him two days ago. / I had seen him two days ago.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ Have you ever been to Mexico? ¦ Did you ever been to Mexico? / Have you ever went to Mexico? / Were you ever been to Mexico?
order ¦ She has achieved a lot in her career. ¦ Ha logrado mucho en su carrera.
order ¦ I haven't seen him since last summer. ¦ No lo he visto desde el verano pasado.
translate ¦ ¿Has terminado el informe? ¦ Have you finished the report? ¦ Have you finished the report yet?
translate ¦ Lo conocí en 2015. ¦ I met him in 2015. ¦ I met her in 2015.
match ¦ so far=hasta ahora; attempt=intento; qualification=título; proud=orgulloso; failure=fracaso

=== 5
title: How long have you been…? ¦ ¿Cuánto tiempo llevas…?
desc: El present perfect continuo con for y since
gram: present perfect continuous: have been + -ing ¦ for / since ¦ How long…?
ref: b1-perfect-continuous
words: since, for, recently, lately, constantly, continuous, continuously, wait, queue, learn, practise, train, revise, exhausted, tiring, stressed, sweat, workout, fitness, gym, jogging, yoga, muscle, effort, struggle, improve, improvement, progress, steady
intro:
<h3>Present perfect continuo</h3>
<p><b>have / has been + -ing</b>. Para una actividad que <b>empezó en el pasado y continúa</b> (o acaba de terminar), con énfasis en la <b>duración</b>:</p>
<div class="example">I<strong>'ve been learning</strong> English <strong>for</strong> three years. (llevo tres años aprendiendo)<br>It<strong>'s been raining since</strong> this morning.<br><strong>How long have</strong> you <strong>been waiting</strong>? — For about twenty minutes.<br>You look tired. — I<strong>'ve been running</strong>. (acabo de parar; se nota el resultado)</div>
<div class="warning">El español usa presente o «llevar + gerundio»: «<b>Vivo</b> aquí desde 2015», «<b>Llevo</b> dos horas esperando». El inglés <b>no</b> admite presente: ✗ I live here since 2015 → ✓ I<b>'ve lived</b> / I<b>'ve been living</b> here since 2015.</div>
<h3>for y since</h3>
<table><tr><td><strong>for</strong> + periodo</td><td>for two hours, for ages, for a long time</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>since</strong> + punto de partida</td><td>since 2010, since Monday, since I was a child</td></tr></table>
<h3>¿Simple o continuo?</h3>
<table><tr><th>Present perfect simple</th><th>Present perfect continuo</th></tr>
<tr><td>resultado, cantidad terminada</td><td>actividad, duración</td></tr>
<tr><td>I<strong>'ve read</strong> three books this month.</td><td>I<strong>'ve been reading</strong> all afternoon.</td></tr>
<tr><td>verbos de estado: I<strong>'ve known</strong> her for years.</td><td>✗ I've been knowing her</td></tr></table>
ex:
fill ¦ I've been waiting ___ an hour! ¦ for ¦ since / during / from ¦ ¡Llevo una hora esperando! ¦ for + periodo.
fill ¦ She's been working here ___ 2019. ¦ since ¦ for / from / during ¦ Trabaja aquí desde 2019. ¦ since + punto de partida.
fill ¦ How long ___ you been learning Japanese? ¦ have ¦ do / are / did ¦ ¿Cuánto tiempo llevas estudiando japonés?
fill ¦ I've ___ her since we were at school. ¦ known ¦ been knowing / knew / know ¦ La conozco desde que íbamos al colegio. ¦ know no va en continuo.
choose ¦ «Vivo en Madrid desde hace diez años» ¦ I've lived in Madrid for ten years. ¦ I live in Madrid since ten years. / I'm living in Madrid for ten years. / I lived in Madrid since ten years.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ I've written five emails this morning. ¦ I've been writing five emails this morning. / I'm writing five emails since this morning. / I wrote five emails since this morning.
order ¦ It has been raining all day. ¦ Ha estado lloviendo todo el día.
order ¦ How long have you been living here? ¦ ¿Cuánto tiempo llevas viviendo aquí?
translate ¦ Llevo dos horas estudiando. ¦ I've been studying for two hours. ¦ I have been studying for two hours.
translate ¦ Lo conozco desde hace años. ¦ I've known him for years. ¦ I have known him for years.
match ¦ exhausted=agotado; struggle=esforzarse, lucha; steady=constante; workout=entrenamiento; revise=repasar

=== 6
title: What's next? ¦ ¿Qué viene ahora?
desc: Formas de futuro y grados de probabilidad
gram: will / going to / present continuous ¦ might / may ¦ be likely to, definitely, probably
ref: b1-future
words: future, likely, unlikely, probably, definitely, certainly, possibly, possibility, probability, predict, prediction, forecast, intend, intention, plan, arrange, expect, hopefully, chance, certain, uncertain, doubt, unpredictable, career, promotion, retire, emigrate, settle, abroad, decide
intro:
<h3>Repaso de las formas de futuro</h3>
<table><tr><th>Forma</th><th>Uso</th><th>Ejemplo</th></tr>
<tr><td>present continuous</td><td>plan organizado (fecha, entradas…)</td><td>I<strong>'m flying</strong> to Berlin on Friday.</td></tr>
<tr><td>be going to</td><td>intención; predicción con pruebas</td><td>I<strong>'m going to</strong> look for a new job.</td></tr>
<tr><td>will</td><td>predicción, decisión en el momento, promesa</td><td>You<strong>'ll</strong> love it. I<strong>'ll</strong> call you.</td></tr>
<tr><td>present simple</td><td>horarios y programas</td><td>The train <strong>leaves</strong> at 7.15.</td></tr></table>
<h3>Probabilidad</h3>
<table><tr><td>100%</td><td>I <strong>will definitely</strong> come. / I'm <strong>certain</strong> to…</td></tr>
<tr><td>80%</td><td>I'll <strong>probably</strong> come. / I'm <strong>likely to</strong> come.</td></tr>
<tr><td>50%</td><td>I <strong>might / may</strong> come. (puede que vaya)</td></tr>
<tr><td>20%</td><td>I <strong>probably won't</strong> come. / I'm <strong>unlikely to</strong> come.</td></tr>
<tr><td>0%</td><td>I <strong>definitely won't</strong> come.</td></tr></table>
<div class="tip"><b>might / may</b> + forma base, sin to: <i>It might rain</i> (✗ it might to rain, ✗ it mights). Posición de los adverbios: <i>She'll <b>probably</b> pass</i>, pero <i>She <b>probably won't</b> pass</i>.</div>
<div class="warning">«Puede que llueva» no es «can»: ✗ It can rain tomorrow → ✓ It <b>might / may</b> rain tomorrow.</div>
ex:
fill ¦ Take an umbrella. It ___ rain later. ¦ might ¦ can / mights / might to ¦ Llévate un paraguas. Puede que llueva más tarde. ¦ Posibilidad → might.
fill ¦ The film ___ at 8.30, so let's meet at 8. ¦ starts ¦ is starting to / will starting / start ¦ La película empieza a las 8.30, así que quedamos a las 8. ¦ Horarios → presente simple.
fill ¦ She's very ill. She ___ probably come to work tomorrow. ¦ won't ¦ doesn't / isn't / not will ¦ Está muy enferma. Probablemente no venga a trabajar mañana.
fill ¦ Prices are ___ to rise next year. ¦ likely ¦ probably / possible / sure that ¦ Es probable que los precios suban el año que viene.
choose ¦ «Puede que me mude a Londres» ¦ I might move to London. ¦ I can move to London. / I might to move to London. / I may moving to London.
choose ¦ ¿Cuál es correcta? ¦ I'll definitely be there. ¦ I definitely will to be there. / I'll be definitely there to. / I'm definitely there will.
order ¦ We're having a party on Saturday night. ¦ El sábado por la noche damos una fiesta.
order ¦ I don't think he'll get the job. ¦ No creo que consiga el trabajo.
translate ¦ Puede que no venga. ¦ She might not come. ¦ He might not come. / She may not come. / He may not come.
translate ¦ El tren sale a las nueve. ¦ The train leaves at nine. ¦ The train leaves at 9. / The train departs at nine.
match ¦ likely=probable; unlikely=improbable; intend=tener intención; settle=instalarse; emigrate=emigrar

=== 7
title: If I were you… ¦ Yo que tú…
desc: Condicionales cero, primero y segundo
gram: zero and first conditional ¦ second conditional: if + past, would ¦ If I were you…
ref: b1-conditionals
words: if, unless, otherwise, provided, imagine, imaginary, situation, dream, wish, lottery, fortune, millionaire, invisible, island, travel, quit, afford, rich, wealthy, poverty, decision, choice, option, advice, consequence, realistic, ideal, perfect, pretend, risk
intro:
<h3>Condicional cero y primero (repaso)</h3>
<table><tr><td><b>cero</b>: verdades generales</td><td>If you <strong>heat</strong> water to 100°, it <strong>boils</strong>.</td></tr>
<tr><td><b>primero</b>: situaciones reales y posibles</td><td>If it <strong>rains</strong>, we<strong>'ll stay</strong> at home.</td></tr></table>
<h3>Segundo condicional</h3>
<p>Para situaciones <b>imaginarias, improbables o contrarias a la realidad</b> en el presente o el futuro:</p>
<table><tr><th>if + pasado simple</th><th>would + verbo</th></tr>
<tr><td>If I <strong>won</strong> the lottery,</td><td>I<strong>'d travel</strong> around the world.</td></tr>
<tr><td>If she <strong>had</strong> more time,</td><td>she<strong>'d learn</strong> Japanese.</td></tr>
<tr><td>If I <strong>were</strong> you,</td><td>I<strong>'d</strong> accept the job.</td></tr></table>
<div class="tip">Es el «si tuviera…, viajaría» del español: el inglés usa el <b>pasado simple</b> donde el español usa el imperfecto de subjuntivo. Con <i>be</i> se prefiere <b>were</b> para todas las personas (<i>If I were you</i>), aunque en lenguaje coloquial se oye <i>was</i>.</div>
<h3>¿Primero o segundo?</h3>
<div class="example">If I <strong>get</strong> the job, I<strong>'ll</strong> buy a car. (es posible: me han entrevistado)<br>If I <strong>got</strong> the job, I<strong>'d</strong> buy a car. (lo veo poco probable)</div>
<div class="warning">Nunca <i>would</i> después de <i>if</i>: ✗ If I would have money → ✓ If I <b>had</b> money. <b>'d</b> puede ser <i>would</i> o <i>had</i>: <i>I'd go</i> = I would go.</div>
ex:
fill ¦ If I ___ a lot of money, I'd buy a house by the sea. ¦ had ¦ would have / have / will have ¦ Si tuviera mucho dinero, me compraría una casa junto al mar. ¦ if + pasado.
fill ¦ If I were you, I ___ talk to her. ¦ 'd ¦ 'll / will / am ¦ Yo que tú, hablaría con ella.

<div class="warning">✗ the same than → ✓ the same <b>as</b> · ✗ similar than → ✓ similar <b>to</b> · ✗ very bigger → ✓ <b>much</b> bigger · ✗ more and more big → ✓ bigger and bigger.</div>

fill ¦ The more you read, ___ you learn. ¦ the more ¦ more / the most / most ¦ Cuanto más lees, más aprendes.
fill ¦ This test was ___ easier than the last one. ¦ much ¦ very / more / most ¦ Este examen fue mucho más fácil que el anterior. ¦ much + comparativo.
fill ¦ Your bag is the same ___ mine. ¦ as ¦ than / like / that ¦ Tu bolso es igual que el mío.
fill ¦ Life is getting more ___ more expensive. ¦ and ¦ or / than / as ¦ La vida es cada vez más cara.
choose ¦ «El portugués se parece al español» ¦ Portuguese is similar to Spanish. ¦ Portuguese is similar than Spanish. / Portuguese is similar as Spanish. / Portuguese is same as Spanish.
choose ¦ «Es el doble de caro» ¦ It's twice as expensive. ¦ It's two times more expensive as. / It's double expensive. / It's twice expensive than.
order ¦ The weather is getting colder and colder. ¦ Cada vez hace más frío.
order ¦ Unlike her sister, she hates sport. ¦ A diferencia de su hermana, odia el deporte.
translate ¦ Cuanto antes, mejor. ¦ The sooner, the better.
translate ¦ Es un poco más alto que yo. ¦ He's a bit taller than me. ¦ He is a bit taller than me. / He's slightly taller than me. / He's a little taller than me. / He is a little taller than me.
